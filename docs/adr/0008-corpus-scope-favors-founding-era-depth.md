# Cycle 004 adds founding-era jurisdictions and the early federal reporters ahead of large modern reporters

The tradition matrix has 2 favorable householder cases before 1860 across
four states, and the federal history-and-tradition test weighs 1791 and 1868
most heavily. The states ranked for cycle 004 by treatise anchors and STR
salience (Illinois, Missouri, California, Ohio) produce little or nothing
before 1860. We decided cycle 004 is Massachusetts, Connecticut, New Jersey,
California, and Ohio, plus a federal tradition set of Federal Cases (circuit
courts 1779-1894), United States Reports, and the D.C. reporters including
the Supreme Court of the District of Columbia (1801-1893), fetched by
reporter rather than by the "U.S." jurisdiction so the 1.84M-case federal
corpus is not pulled wholesale. English Reports (CommonLII) are ingested
alongside as their own partition. Illinois and Missouri are deferred, and
the full federal reporters (F., F.2d, F.3d) are reached through the
citation graph and the hand-built argument-side file, not ingested.

## Considered options

- Keep the five states decided on 2026-08-31: breadth without era depth.
- All federal cases: 1.84M cases of mostly irrelevant material; the
  tradition value sits in the early reporters and D.C.

## Consequences

D.C. was declined on 2026-08-31 and is now included on the merits: it is
founding-era-adjacent federal common law and the home jurisdiction of the
record's first consumer. Colonial and early state statutes on lodging and
innkeeping are in scope as a small hand-curated source, not a pipeline
build.
